## Overall judgement

Counts: 60 defects. 1 major, 18 minor, 41 nit. Every chapter was read in full. Its solutions file was read in full and recomputed, and its figure pages were looked at. The book is sound: the exercise and problem answers are right in almost every case, which is rare. Calibration holds everywhere: 12 exercises as 4/5/3; weekend problems of 24 questions, 25 in chapters 3, 24 and 29; chapter 30 is a project. The defects sit in explanatory prose, captions and a few printed programs, not in the answers. None of Book 1's trap classes appears in a printed listing: no TikZ grid or radius scaling, no ruff or sys.path headers, and no exercise copied from its worked example. The one cut range is R3-048.

**Part I, C (ch. 1–7).** This part is strong, and it holds the book's one major defect. Chapter 2 states the usual arithmetic conversions without the rule for a signed type of greater rank, and its figure would then send `long` vs `unsigned` the wrong way, against the chapter's own run (R3-001). Its overflow exercise and problem also overclaim (R3-002, R3-003). The other weaknesses are habits, not errors:
- printed C that uses `malloc` results unchecked (R3-006), which chapter 5 itself teaches against;
- unledgered history (R3-008);
- two overclaims about opaque types (R3-010, R3-011).

Chapter 2 needs R3-001 to R3-003 fixed before anything else in the book.

**Part II, tools (ch. 8–9).** This part is sound: the problem numbers were recomputed from the generated logs. There is one factual error, the `pipefail` semantics (R3-015).

**Part III, reasoning (ch. 10–13).** The proofs are careful, and the induction and correctness chapters are among the best in the book. Chapter 13's limit claims about Θ classes contradict its own problem (R3-022).

**Part IV, data structures (ch. 14–19).** The content is good and every trace is right. The defects are in what surrounds the code:
- prose that misquotes timing outputs (R3-024);
- a chapter test with a use-after-free and a leak, which only passes because `tools/test_code.sh` builds C tests without sanitizers (R3-027);
- an LLRB solution that borrows the classic red-black bounds (R3-032);
- wrong-sense term links (R3-037).

**Part V, algorithm design (ch. 20–26).** All of this part was verified:
- the Huffman, chain, knapsack, town-graph, MST and spanning-tree-count numbers were recomputed;
- one arithmetic derivation is wrong, although its total is right (R3-035, quickselect);
- one slack figure misreads its own output (R3-043).

**Part VI, machine and data (ch. 27–30).** Chapter 27 has no defect. In chapter 28, the control-unit caption miscounts the cycles per instruction (R3-050). Chapter 29 is clean apart from two nits. Chapter 30 is a good capstone, and every number in it was reproduced by regenerating the corpus. Two defects there:
- its timing chart is drawn from a stale run (R3-056);
- its C repeats the `p = realloc(p, …)` pattern that chapter 5 teaches against (R3-058).

**Patterns.**
1. Prose or captions misquote the output or code they describe: R3-024, R3-043, R3-050, R3-054, R3-056 and the timing nits.
2. Solutions and proofs overclaim with "exactly", "iff", "at most" or "tends to" where only a bound or a special case holds: R3-002, R3-003, R3-022, R3-032.
3. Printed C leaves `malloc`/`realloc` results unchecked, against chapter 5's own rule: R3-006, R3-058.
4. C tests are not built under sanitizers, which hid R3-027.
   Recommendation: add `-fsanitize=address,undefined` to the C test build in `tools/test_code.sh`. It found nothing else when I ran it over the book.
5. History boxes make checkable claims with no ledger row (R3-008 and nits).
6. The term linker matches words, not meanings (R3-037).
7. Figures or data files are not regenerated after a recapture (R3-056).

**Weak chapters.** None is weak overall. Chapter 2 needs the conversion-rule rewrite, with its figure and the two overflow items. Chapters 14 and 15 need their prose re-matched to their outputs, and the test fix.
