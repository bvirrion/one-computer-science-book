## Overall judgement

**Counts: 61 defects, of which 1 major, 21 minor and 39 nits.** All 37 chapters and the typing appendix were read, along with every solution file. Every exercise solution and weekend problem was recomputed along its printed path, and every "predict the output" exercise was run by hand. No solution gives a wrong final answer. The CS content is sound and well pitched. The book teaches the machine before Python, as planned, and Python does not appear before grade 6. Nothing personal and no curriculum name is printed. The account prints as `ada` and the machine as `laptop`. Security stays defensive (ciphers broken only for teaching, no attacks on real systems). The defects are mostly in how things are **shown**: figures drawn at a scaled unit, listing line ranges that cut the last line, and captions that describe a different output. They are not in what is taught.

**Patterns** (each fix is mechanical once known):

1. **Scaled TikZ grids** (R1-015, major; R1-023). `grid` without `step` inside a picture with `x=`/`y=` draws 1 cm lines under unit-placed content, and `circle (r)` takes its radius in cm. This hits every robot, maze and pixel grid that children are asked to count.
