## Overall judgement

All 32 chapters and their solutions were read in book order. 63 defects: **4 major, 20 minor, 39 nit**.
Every printed number I recomputed agrees with the captured outputs, except where a defect row says otherwise.
I found nothing forbidden in print:
- no curriculum or university name;
- nothing personal: the captures print `ada` and `laptop`;
- no working exploit: chapter 29 only overwrites a flag in its own toy program, with ASLR off;
- the toy cryptography is labelled as toy.

The book is strong. Its listings, outputs and solutions hold together far better than a typical third-year text. The defects are mostly in the prose around the outputs, not in the code.

**Part I, compilers (ch. 1–9).** This part has three of the four majors:
- R5-007: step counts misquoted against the chapter's own output;
- R5-010: an edge in the control-flow graph of `fib` is wrong;
- R5-012: a false minimality claim in the admitted phi-placement theorem.

Chapter 8 is the weakest chapter of the part, with five defects, two of them solution slips (R5-013, R5-014). Chapter 6 states Curry–Howard without its normalisation hypothesis (R5-008). Chapters 2, 3 and 7 are otherwise very careful.

**Part II, operating systems (ch. 10–14).** Chapters 11 and 12 are clean. Chapter 10 has two captions that contradict its own scheduling outputs (R5-019, R5-020). Chapter 13 has the fourth major, R5-023: release/acquire pairs are not enough for sequential consistency in general. The coordinator confirmed it in triage. Chapter 14 has only a nit.

**Part III, theory (ch. 15–20).** This is the best-written mathematics in the book; chapters 15, 16 and 19 have nits only. Chapter 17 is the weak one:
- R5-029: an exercise whose stated assignment does not work;
- R5-030: a false step in a solution;
- three nits on proof details.

Chapter 20 has one caption that overclaims (R5-038).

**Part IV, data and distributed systems (ch. 21–26).** Every table I recomputed checks. Chapter 25 is the weakest chapter of the book's second half:
- R5-048: a gap in the two-generals proof;
- R5-049: a caption range that the snapshots do not show;
- R5-050: two cases swapped.

Other minors in this part:
- chapter 23: the "SSI" check lets Fekete's read-only anomaly through (R5-044), shown by running `mvcc.py`;
- chapter 26: exercise 3's logs violate log matching (R5-052);
- chapter 24: the losses on the hybrid-core laptop are blamed on the wrong cause (R5-045, unverified).

**Part V, security (ch. 27–29).** Accurate and careful. Chapter 28 is strong. There is one minor in chapter 29: the ASLR solution says the stack keeps its offset within a page, and the output shows that it does not (R5-057). The rest are nits.

**Part VI, building systems (ch. 30–32).** Chapter 31 is very clean, with one nit, and chapter 32 is excellent:
- the WAL bytes match the `od` dump;
- the replay and locality proofs are sound;
- the bench model recomputes.

Chapter 30 has one minor: a branch-coverage explanation contradicts its own output (R5-059).
