## Overall judgement

**Counts: 111 defects in all: 5 major, 36 minor, 70 nit.** All 31 chapters were read in full, solutions included, and every figure page was rendered. Every weekend problem and every exercise output was checked against its printed run.

The book is sound. The CS and the mathematics are right almost everywhere. The code is idiomatic and mostly whole, and the outputs reproduce. The proofs at this level (Armstrong, Heath, Kőnig/Hall, max-flow min-cut, alpha–beta, k-means termination, quicksort's expectation) are complete and correct. The defects that matter are of five kinds:

- solutions that misquote the run they print;
- a few overclaimed theorems;
- figures that disagree with their code or caption;
- listings cut short of what the output shows;
- above all, a book-wide term-linker fault that sends ordinary English words to unrelated definitions.

**Part I — C++ (ch. 1–5).** Solid teaching of RAII, copy/move and templates. It has two of the five majors. The first is a wrong self-move explanation (R4-005). The second is a solution and caption that contradict the printed erase output (R4-017). There are also a leaked-object count (R4-006), a NRVO hook that would print the same without NRVO (R4-008), a vtable figure whose slot numbers vanish (R4-011), and a miscounted instantiation list (R4-015). **Weakest part per page.** Ch. 2 needs its solutions re-derived from the printed outputs.

**Part II — Rust and functional programming (ch. 6–9).** Good, idiomatic code. The issues are claims a little stronger than the truth: the aliasing proposition (R4-019), "one vtable per type" (R4-024), "vectorised" f64 sum (R4-025), a units-figure arrow (R4-026), and a path-copying figure that is not a search tree (R4-029). Ch. 8 needs its three propositions and its figure checked against the code.

**Part III — Architecture (ch. 10–12).** The content is correct. The weak spot is printed ranges that cut the code the exercises ask about: exo_ra.S and switch_table.S (R4-074, R4-077, R4-078). Ch. 12 over-reads two measurements (R4-035, R4-038).

**Part IV — Systems (ch. 13–16).** Strong on processes, pipes and threads. It defines "process", "system call", "race condition" and "lock" a second time after Book 2, which already owns them (R4-040, R4-045). The philosophers figure is mis-indexed (R4-046). Ch. 16's final answer quotes rates that are not the run printed (R4-049, major). Ch. 16's problem solution needs re-checking line by line against designs.dat.

**Part V — Networks (ch. 17–20).** Accurate protocols and good problems. Ch. 17's CRC solution claims a protection the plain-division CRC-16 does not give (R4-051, major). Ch. 18 has a wrong count-to-infinity claim (R4-055), and ch. 20 a DNS budget row that undercounts a query (R4-064). Wrong-target links cluster here: "broadcast address", "backoff".

**Part VI — Languages and logic (ch. 21–24).** The automata chapters are clean apart from one product-figure edge (R4-066) and a pumping-length exercise (R4-067). Ch. 23 says a guessing PDA "never guesses" (R4-072). **Ch. 24 is the weakest chapter in the book.** It states refutation-completeness for binary resolution without factoring, which is false, and its code implements exactly that rule (R4-080, major). It also has the densest wrong-target links ("borrow" ×9, "bound", "complete", "atomic"; R4-081) and four small slips (R4-082 to R4-086).
